Abstract

The Hubble tension admits fundamentally different interpretations: a physical expansion rate inflated by local kinematics (e.g., a Gpc-scale underdensity), or an operational redshift-distance calibration artifact. This paper tests the kinematic void hypothesis against the Temporal Equivalence Principle (TEP), which models cosmological redshift as accumulated temporal shear on a static spatial manifold. Using the matched $z \ge 0.05$ submatrix of the native Pantheon+ STAT+SYS covariance, the published KBC/MOND gradual-decay profiles are strongly rejected ($\Delta\chi^2 > 100$). A calibration-independent ratio $R_H = 1.009 \pm 0.006$ excludes the predicted declines — $4.3\%$ (Gaussian profile) and $5.1\%$ (Exponential) — at $8.4\sigma$ and $9.7\sigma$ respectively, falsifying the published KBC/MOND gradual-decay void solution on its own native coordinates. While this competitor exclusion carries no direct evidential weight for TEP by itself, the residual directional and environmental data are consistent with the independent clock-transport predictions of TEP. An exploratory fit to pre-standardization Pantheon+ residuals has an interior optimum at $L_T=55.6$ Mpc along the pre-specified CF4 axis, surviving a 300-draw permutation look-elsewhere calibration with zero null exceedances ($-2\Delta\ln L = 24.0$ versus a null maximum of $10.8$, setting a Monte Carlo resolution-limited bound $p = 1/301 \approx 0.0033$, or $\gtrsim 2.7\sigma$), though CF4 cannot independently distinguish the kernel from its $1/r$ asymptote. However, the identified coherence scale places the characteristic structure in the tens-of-Mpc local-environment regime rather than the Gpc scale required by the KBC void. Because the standard rest-frame time mapping used by SALT3 implicitly assumes that the spectroscopic redshift fully accounts for the transformation between observed and emitted light-curve timescales, aggregate SALT3 standardization attenuates the pre-standardization directional signal by $70 \pm 16\%$, consistent with partial absorption of a temporal contribution. The underlying temporal shear instead manifests locally: host-galaxy systemic redshifts are weighted toward deeper central potentials than disk standard candles, artificially contracting rest-frame timescales. Consequently, Cepheid distances are systematically shorter than TRGB distances within the same galaxies ($2.39\sigma$ directional preference), and single-galaxy Period-Luminosity gradients within the LMC ($3.30\sigma$, robust to confounder controls) and M31 ($3.65\sigma$ baseline in the HST PHAT catalogue, $2.18\sigma$ period–colour matched, and $6.17\sigma$ under flux-independent neighbour-density crowding control; Paper 11) are directionally consistent with the predicted clock-rate differential; potential-scaling tests across independent samples are presently leverage-limited rather than excluded. The results are consistent with a clock-transport contribution across the distance-ladder measurement chain driven by localized temporal topology, and motivate the independent environmental and spectroscopic tests specified below.
