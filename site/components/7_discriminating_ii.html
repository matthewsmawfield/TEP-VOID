6. Consequence II: Peculiar Velocity Calibration Sensitivity

This section presents a deterministic consequence and consistency check, not an independent high-significance test. It illustrates how the indicator-specific distance divergence established in Section 5 propagates into the peculiar velocity field. Peculiar velocities are not directly observed; they are derived quantities, computed as $v_{\rm pec} = cz - H_0 d$, where $d$ is the distance inferred from a standard candle and $H_0$ is the assumed background expansion rate used to subtract the Hubble flow. If the distance calibration is biased, the inferred peculiar velocity field is systematically distorted.

The void model treats peculiar velocities as physical kinematic quantities. Under this framework, the $4.8\sigma$ bulk-flow anomaly in CosmicFlows-4 is independent evidence for the KBC void: galaxies are physically moving outward, and this motion is captured in the velocity field regardless of which distance indicator is used.

The TEP framework predicts that the Cepheid distance compression (Section 5; sign test $2.39\sigma$, Xi regression correct negative sign on TEP-H0 raw data) produces a calibration-dependent systematic shift in the peculiar-velocity field. The Cepheid-calibrated $H_0 = 73.0$ exceeds the TRGB-calibrated $H_0 = 69.8$ by $\Delta H_0 = 3.2\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$. When this inflated $H_0$ is used to compute peculiar velocities, the subtraction $cz - H_0 d$ introduces a distance-dependent shift:

\begin{equation} \Delta v_{\rm pec} = v_{\rm pec}^{\rm Cep} - v_{\rm pec}^{\rm TRGB} = (H_0^{\rm TRGB} - H_0^{\rm Cep}) \times d = -3.2 \times d\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}} \end{equation}

This shift is deterministic by construction (it is not a statistical test), but it quantifies the physical link between the Cepheid calibration bias and the bulk-flow anomaly. Using the full 38,053-entry CosmicFlows-4 group catalog, the shift reaches $-640\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ at 200 Mpc and $-800\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ at 250 Mpc. The RMS shift across the catalog is $839\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, and 89% of galaxies have calibration-induced shifts exceeding $200\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ — comparable to the typical peculiar velocity dispersion.

The bulk-flow amplitude is correspondingly sensitive: the mean reduction in the TRGB-calibrated bulk flow relative to the Cepheid-calibrated value is 3.3% across radial bins from 50 to 250 Mpc. Under the void model, the bulk flow is gravitational and should not depend on the $H_0$ calibration. Under TEP, the 3.3% reduction is the expected consequence of the indicator divergence (Section 5): the Cepheid-calibrated $H_0$ inflates the apparent bulk flow, and using the TRGB calibration partially removes this inflation.

The $4.8\sigma$ Watkins et al. (2023) bulk-flow anomaly is therefore not purely kinematic evidence for a local void. The velocity catalog is calibration-sensitive: switching from Cepheid-anchored to TRGB-anchored $H_0$ changes the measured bulk-flow amplitude by $3.3\%$. This directly bounds the level of distance-scale leakage into the bulk-flow statistic. The void model does not naturally predict this calibration dependence; TEP provides a natural consequence of the acoustic clock bias. However, Mazurenko et al. (2024) note that the properly estimated bulk-flow vector, unlike an individual peculiar velocity, is largely insensitive to the assumed $H_0$. The $3.3\%$ change reported here is computed from a vector average of recalculated peculiar velocities, not from the full Watkins/Mazurenko bulk-flow estimator. This result should therefore be interpreted as a prediction of calibration sensitivity, not as standalone observed evidence for TEP. The primary evidential weight in this paper rests on Observable III (the direct $\mu(z)$ rejection) and the calibration-independent $R_H$ test.

6.1 Host-Potential-Dependent Velocity Shift: A Calibration-Sensitivity Diagnostic

The constant $\Delta v = -3.2 \times d$ shift above uses a single $\Delta H_0$ applied uniformly. The full TEP prediction is sharper: the velocity shift should correlate with host gravitational potential, because the Cepheid distance compression $\Delta\mu_i = \kappa_{\rm Cep} \cdot X_i$ is host-potential-dependent. The resulting peculiar velocity shift is

\begin{equation} \Delta v_{{\rm pec},i} = -H_0^{\rm Cep} d_i\left(10^{\kappa_{\rm Cep} X_i/5} - 1\right) \approx -H_0^{\rm Cep} d_i\,\frac{\ln 10}{5}\,\kappa_{\rm Cep} X_i \end{equation}

where $X_i = (S_{\rm total}\,u_\phi^2 - U_{\rm ref})/c^2$ is the screened TEP potential coordinate (Section 5.5). Since $\Delta v_i$ is constructed proportionally to $X_i$, a correlation between $\Delta v_i$ and any variable entering $X_i$ is partly built in by design. This is a prediction of the TEP framework, not independent observed evidence. Confirming it requires correlating actual velocity residuals — not TEP-constructed shifts — with $X_i$. The qualitative distinction from a kinematic void (which produces a distance-dependent shift independent of host mass) remains valid as a prediction.

Using the matched calibrator sample with measured potentials, the host-potential-dependent shift is of order a few tens of km/s (e.g., approximately $-54$ to $+16$ km/s for the 18 galaxies with potential data), not hundreds or thousands of km/s. Because $\Delta v_i$ is constructed proportionally to $X_i$, any correlation with variables that enter $X_i$ is partly built in by design. A kinematic void model predicts no such host-mass dependence, so a future correlation of actual velocity residuals with $X_i$ would still distinguish the two frameworks; the present estimate is a calibration-sensitivity diagnostic, not a discriminating observation. A rigorous test requires a larger sample with homogeneous Cepheid reductions and TRGB cross-checks, the subject of ongoing work.
